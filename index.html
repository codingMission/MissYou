Chapter three · A story from another century

The mapmaker's workshop.

“In another century, I think you would have been a mapmaker.”

People would come to ask for directions. And somehow leave with a map, three philosophical questions, and advice they never asked for.

You'd argue with scholars, question merchants, collect stories, and refuse to accept nonsense just because someone said it confidently.

I can imagine you making your mother proud and contributing something meaningful to Turkey, one excellent question at a time.

And the cat would probably own the shop.

A small imaginary port city. You, naturally, are in charge of the workshop.

Chapter four · For the days that are simply days

You don't have to shine every day to remain yourself.

Some days are bright.
Some days are complicated.
Some days you don't feel much like yourself.

You don't have to be cheerful to be worth listening to.

You don't have to turn a difficult day into a funny story.

And you don't have to apologize for needing time.

I like hearing from you, Selen. Not just when life is exciting. On ordinary days, too. And on the difficult ones, whenever you feel like sharing.

Chapter six · A note from Morocco

Dear Selen,

I wanted to make you something that wasn't another ordinary message.

Not because there's a special occasion, and definitely not because I expect anything in return. I just wanted to give a little shape to something I've been thinking about.

It's strange how someone can become familiar through letters. You begin with introductions, and somehow, after a while, you recognize the little details of their world.

I remember your university stories, your excitement about sociology, your strange encounters with campus wildlife, your creative experiments, and the way you can turn an ordinary afternoon into a story worth reading.

And I remember that not every day has been easy.

I hope you know you don't have to be the funny, energetic version of yourself every time we talk. You can be tired. You can be frustrated. You can have absolutely nothing interesting to report.

I'd still be glad to hear from you.

I miss our conversations, Selen. The thoughtful ones, the ridiculous ones, and the ones that somehow become both.

I made this little place because I wanted you to see how much those small moments have meant to me.

And, for the record: Heart over mind. Some people stay in your thoughts even during the busiest days.

No expectations. No deadlines. Just something for you to visit whenever you feel like it.

I'm glad our paths crossed.
And I'm glad I got to know you.

— Saad
